**0:00–0:10 · You** (your 10-second clip, phone held sideways; or say this over the title card)
"Hi, I'm [Name], a ninth grader at [School]. This is Minute Zero, an app for anyone who sees someone
collapse and needs to help."

**0:10–0:25 · The first minutes**
"When someone collapses, the next few minutes decide what happens. But in a panic, most people forget
what to do, and when they did it."

**0:25–0:45 · The problem**
"911 dispatchers can coach CPR over the phone, but that help has been inconsistent: in one national
survey, 46% of the 911 centers that answered gave no instructions for medical emergencies. And paramedics
need the times: when the person collapsed, when CPR started, when stroke symptoms began."

**0:45–1:20 · One tap to start**
"Minute Zero works alongside your 911 call. One tap starts a clock and a log of everything you do, with
the time. Call 911 and Text 911 are always at the top. If someone isn't responding or breathing normally,
it goes straight to CPR. The beat keeps you at 110 compressions a minute, a timer reminds you to switch
rescuers every two minutes, and one tap logs when the AED arrives."

**1:20–1:45 · Stroke check**
"For a possible stroke, it walks you through the B.E. F.A.S.T. warning signs and records when the
symptoms started, the time doctors need most. If you don't know what's happening, a few yes-or-no
questions point you to the right steps. The app never diagnoses; it only guides."

**1:45–2:10 · Paramedic handoff**
"When paramedics arrive, they get a clear summary: the key times, how long ago each one was, and the full
timeline. You can show it full screen, copy it or share it. It also finds your location to read to the
dispatcher."

**2:10–2:26 · Made for real life**
"You can save family members' conditions, so their situation is at the top and their info goes to the
paramedics. There's large text, a calm mode and a quick tutorial."

**2:26–2:48 · Works offline**
"I wrote it in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so it runs in any phone browser, and a service worker lets it
work offline. Every medical step comes from the American Heart Association, the Red Cross, the American
Stroke Association or Stop the Bleed. I used Claude, an AI assistant, as a coding partner."

**2:48–2:58 · Close**
"I learned that in an emergency, simple beats clever. Minute Zero: so someone remembers every minute."
