# Minute Zero — demo video script (about 3 minutes)

Check the length limit in the submission form first; this script runs about 3:00 when read at a calm pace.
**SCREEN** = what to show. **SAY** = what to read. Replace `[Your name]`.

## Before you record
- **Never tap Call 911 or Text 911 on a real phone while recording.** On a phone it really opens the dialer
  (and 911 works even in airplane mode). Point at the button instead, or record those parts on a computer,
  where the app shows its "on a computer" message.
- Do Not Disturb on, brightness up, volume up (the CPR beat should be heard).
- Start from a fresh app: ☰ → About → delete saved data, then reload.
- Allow location once beforehand (☰ → Settings), so no popup appears on camera.
- Record with the phone's built-in screen recorder; voice over it, or record your voice separately.

## 0:00–0:15 · Hook
**SCREEN:** Home screen with the big START button.
**SAY:** "When someone collapses, the next few minutes decide what happens. But in a panic, most people
forget what to do, and when they did it."

## 0:15–0:35 · The problem
**SCREEN:** Home screen, slowly scroll or hold.
**SAY:** "911 dispatchers can coach CPR over the phone, but that help has been inconsistent. In one national
survey, 46% of the 911 centers that answered gave no instructions for medical emergencies. And when paramedics
arrive, they ask: When did they collapse? When did CPR start? When did the stroke symptoms begin? Those times
decide the treatment, and a scared bystander usually can't answer."

## 0:35–0:45 · Introduction
**SCREEN:** Point at "Works alongside your 911 call".
**SAY:** "I'm [Your name], and I built Minute Zero. It works alongside your 911 call, not instead of it."

## 0:45–1:20 · Start + CPR
**SCREEN:** Tap START. The clock starts. Point at Call 911 at the top (don't tap it).
**SAY:** "One tap starts a clock and a log of everything you do, with the time. Call 911 is always at the top."
**SCREEN:** Tap "Unresponsive / not breathing normally", then START CPR. Let the beat run for 3 seconds.
**SAY:** "If someone isn't responding or breathing normally, it goes straight to CPR. The beat keeps you at 110
compressions a minute. A timer reminds you to switch rescuers every two minutes, and these buttons log when the
AED arrives and when a shock is given."
**SCREEN:** Tap "AED arrived".

## 1:20–1:45 · Stroke + "Not sure?"
**SCREEN:** Change situation → Possible stroke → Show steps. Answer two B.E. F.A.S.T. questions; show the pictures.
**SAY:** "For a possible stroke, it walks you through the B.E. F.A.S.T. warning signs and records when the symptoms
started, the time doctors need most."
**SCREEN:** Briefly show "Not sure? Answer a few questions".
**SAY:** "If you don't know what's happening, a few yes-or-no questions from the official warning signs point you
to the right steps. The app never diagnoses; it only guides."

## 1:45–2:10 · Handoff for paramedics
**SCREEN:** Tap "EMS arrived", then "Handoff for paramedics". Show the key times, then "Show to paramedic".
**SAY:** "When paramedics arrive, they get a clear summary: the key times, how long ago each one was, and the full
timeline. You can show it full screen, copy it or share it."
**SCREEN:** Point at "📍 Your location for 911".
**SAY:** "It can also find your location to read to the dispatcher, and write a Text 911 message for you if you
can't talk."

## 2:10–2:25 · Made for real life
**SCREEN:** Quick cuts: ☰ menu → My people (a person with epilepsy → "Seizure for Dad") → Settings presets → tutorial.
**SAY:** "You can save family members' conditions, so their situation is at the top and their info goes to the
paramedics. There's large text, a calm mode, and a 30-second tutorial."

## 2:25–2:50 · How it's built
**SCREEN:** Turn on airplane mode, reload: the app still opens. Then show About & Sources.
**SAY:** "It's built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so it runs in any phone browser and works offline,
even with no signal. Every medical step comes from the American Heart Association, the Red Cross, the American
Stroke Association or Stop the Bleed, with the source shown under each step. I tested it with automated browser
tests and an accessibility checker."

## 2:50–3:00 · Close
**SCREEN:** Home screen with START.
**SAY:** "Minute Zero: because in an emergency, every minute counts, and someone should remember them."
